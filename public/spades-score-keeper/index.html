How Spades scoring works

Spades is played by two partnerships. Before each hand every player bids the tricks they expect to take, and partners add their bids together into one contract. A side that takes at least its bid scores 10 points a trick bid, plus 1 point for each trick over. A side that takes fewer is set, and loses 10 points a trick bid. The first side to reach 500 wins.

Bags

Each trick taken over the bid is a bag, worth 1 point. Bags carry over from hand to hand, and every time a side's bags reach 10, it loses 100 points. Overbidding costs a hand; underbidding costs bags.

Nil

A player can bid nil: no tricks at all. If they take none, their side scores 100; if they take even one, the side loses 100. Their partner's bid is scored as usual.

An example hand

Your side bids 4 and takes 6: 40 for the bid and 2 bags, so 42. The other side bid 5 and took only 3, so they're set and lose 50, but one of them bid nil and took nothing: 100 for that, and +50 for the hand.

Tables differ: some play to 300, count a nil player's tricks toward the partner's bid, or allow a blind nil. The target can be changed in the Builder; enter the tricks the side took together.

Questions

How do you score Spades?

Partners add their bids into one contract. Make it and score 10 points a trick bid, plus 1 for each trick over (a bag). Fall short and lose 10 points a trick bid. The first side to 500 wins.

What are bags in Spades?

A bag is a trick taken over the bid, worth 1 point. Bags add up from hand to hand, and every 10 bags cost the side 100 points.

How does nil work in Spades?

A player who bids nil promises to take no tricks. Their side scores 100 if they take none and loses 100 if they take any. The partner's own bid is scored separately.

What score does Spades go to?

Most tables play to 500. Some play to 300 or 250 for a shorter game, and the target can be changed.

Can I change the scoring to our house rules?

The target can be changed and saved in the Builder. The bid, bag and nil scoring is the standard one.
