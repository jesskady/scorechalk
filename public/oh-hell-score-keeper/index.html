How Oh Hell scoring works

Oh Hell (also called Oh Heck, Blackout, or Up and Down the River) is played over a run of hands with a different number of cards each time: often one card, up to seven, and back down to one, thirteen hands in all. After the deal every player bids exactly how many tricks they will take. A player who takes exactly their bid scores 10 points plus 1 a trick. Anyone who takes more or fewer scores nothing. After the last hand, the highest total wins.

The dealer's bid

The dealer bids last, and in most games may not bid the number that would make all the bids add up to the tricks in the hand, so at least one player has to miss.

An example hand

In a five-card hand, Alex bids 2 and takes 2: 12. Jo bids 0 and takes none: 10. Sam bids 2 and takes 3, and Kim bids 1 and takes none, so they both score nothing.

Tables play it many ways: more or fewer hands, or a point off for each trick missed. The number of hands can be changed in the Builder; the scoring here is the exact-bid one.

Questions

How do you score Oh Hell?

A player who takes exactly the tricks they bid scores 10 points plus 1 for each trick. A player who takes more or fewer scores nothing for the hand.

How many hands are in Oh Hell?

A common game goes from one card up to seven and back down to one: thirteen hands. Some tables go higher, or only one way.

What is the dealer's rule in Oh Hell?

The dealer bids last and usually can't make the bids add up to the number of tricks in the hand, so not everyone can make their bid.

Is Oh Hell the same as Up and Down the River?

Yes: Oh Hell, Oh Heck, Blackout and Up and Down the River are names for the same exact-bid trick-taking game.

Can I change the scoring to our house rules?

The number of hands can be changed and saved in the Builder. The scoring is the standard exact-bid one: 10 plus 1 a trick.
